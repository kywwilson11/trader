2005: ROBERT AUMANN & THOMAS SCHELLING
"For having enhanced our understanding of conflict and cooperation
through game-theory analysis"
------------------------------------------------------------------------

CONTRIBUTION SUMMARY:
Aumann formalized repeated game theory, showing how cooperation emerges
in repeated interactions even among self-interested agents. Schelling
introduced focal points and strategic behavior analysis with minimal
mathematical formalism, explaining how coordination happens in conflict
and cooperation scenarios.

RELEVANCE TO TRADING: LOW-MODERATE
Game theory has indirect relevance to order execution strategy. When our
bot places orders, it participates in a repeated game with other market
participants (other algos, market makers, institutional traders). Key
insights:

  - REPEATED GAMES: Our bot trades the same symbols daily. Other
    participants may detect and front-run predictable patterns. Aumann
    showed that in repeated games, strategies should incorporate
    randomization to avoid being exploited.

  - FOCAL POINTS (Schelling): In thin markets (crypto especially),
    round numbers ($50,000 BTC, $100 SOL) act as Schelling focal points
    where liquidity clusters. Our limit order logic could target prices
    near these psychological levels.

POTENTIAL IMPLEMENTATION:
  - Add small random jitter to order timing (currently fixed 30s loops)
  - Randomize limit order offset (currently fixed 5bps in order_utils.py)
  - Detect round-number support/resistance levels in price data
  Data needed: None beyond current data
  Complexity: Low
  Impact: Marginal improvement in execution quality
  Feasibility on Jetson: Trivial

2006: EDMUND PHELPS
"For his analysis of intertemporal tradeoffs in macroeconomic policy"
------------------------------------------------------------------------

CONTRIBUTION SUMMARY:
Phelps formalized the expectations-augmented Phillips curve, showing
that there is no long-run tradeoff between inflation and unemployment.
Central bank policy can only temporarily reduce unemployment below its
"natural rate" by creating unexpected inflation. Once expectations
adjust, unemployment returns to its natural level.

RELEVANCE TO TRADING: MODERATE
The key insight is that INFLATION EXPECTATIONS drive market behavior
more than current inflation. This has direct implications for our
macro-environment assessment:

  - When inflation expectations are rising (breakeven rates, TIPS
    spreads), risk assets face headwinds from anticipated tightening.
  - When expectations are falling, looser future policy is anticipated,
    supporting risk assets.
  - The natural rate of unemployment serves as a benchmark: employment
    data significantly below it signals overheating and potential
    tightening.

POTENTIAL IMPLEMENTATION:
  - Add inflation expectations data (TIPS breakeven rates) as an input
    to the LLM analyst's macro context
  - Use FRED API to fetch 5Y and 10Y breakeven inflation rates
  - When breakevens are rising sharply, increase the LLM's bearish bias
  Data needed: FRED API (free, public)
  Complexity: Low (API call + pass to LLM prompt)
  Impact: Better macro regime awareness in LLM scoring
  Feasibility on Jetson: Trivial (HTTP request)

2007: LEONID HURWICZ, ERIC MASKIN & ROGER MYERSON
"For having laid the foundations of mechanism design theory"
------------------------------------------------------------------------

CONTRIBUTION SUMMARY:
Mechanism design is "reverse game theory" -- instead of analyzing
existing games, it asks how to design rules/mechanisms to achieve
desired outcomes. Key concepts include incentive compatibility (making
truthful behavior optimal) and the revelation principle.

RELEVANCE TO TRADING: LOW-MODERATE
Mechanism design is foundational to understanding market microstructure:

  - ORDER TYPES AS MECHANISMS: Different order types (limit, market,
    stop, trailing stop) are mechanisms that reveal different amounts
    of information to the market. Our bot's use of limit orders with
    market fallback is a mechanism design choice -- it initially hides
    urgency (limit) then reveals it if needed (market fallback).

  - AUCTION FORMATS: Stock exchanges use continuous double auctions,
    while crypto exchanges may use different matching engines. The
    choice of venue/exchange affects execution quality.

  - INFORMATION REVELATION: Every order we place reveals information
    about our model's predictions. In thin crypto markets, large
    orders can move prices against us (adverse selection).

POTENTIAL IMPLEMENTATION:
  - Implement order splitting for larger positions to reduce market
    impact (currently, NOTIONAL_PER_SYMBOL = $1000-5000 goes as single
    orders)
  - Add adaptive order type selection based on spread/liquidity
  - Consider iceberg orders if Alpaca supports them
  Data needed: Current spread data (already collected in order_utils.py)
  Complexity: Moderate
  Impact: Better execution prices, especially in illiquid crypto
  Feasibility on Jetson: Easy (logic changes, no compute overhead)

2009: ELINOR OSTROM & OLIVER WILLIAMSON
"For analysis of economic governance" (commons / firm boundaries)
------------------------------------------------------------------------

CONTRIBUTION SUMMARY:
Ostrom showed that common-pool resources can be effectively managed
without privatization or central regulation through community
governance. Williamson analyzed transaction costs as the key
determinant of whether activities are organized within firms or
through markets.

RELEVANCE TO TRADING: LOW
Transaction cost economics has a conceptual connection to trading:

  - TRANSACTION COSTS: Our bot faces transaction costs (spreads,
    slippage, Alpaca commissions) that erode returns. Williamson's
    framework suggests we should only trade when expected profit
    exceeds transaction costs by a meaningful margin.

  - We already partially implement this through the spread check in
    order_utils.py and the prediction threshold in the trading loops.

POTENTIAL IMPLEMENTATION:
  - Explicitly model total transaction costs (spread + slippage +
    opportunity cost) and require predicted returns to exceed them
    by a configurable multiplier
  Data needed: Historical fill data (spread vs. predicted return)
  Complexity: Low
  Impact: Reduced over-trading, fewer low-conviction entries
  Feasibility on Jetson: Trivial

2010: PETER DIAMOND, DALE MORTENSEN & CHRISTOPHER PISSARIDES
"For their analysis of markets with search frictions"
------------------------------------------------------------------------

CONTRIBUTION SUMMARY:
The Diamond-Mortensen-Pissarides (DMP) model analyzes markets where
finding a match is costly and time-consuming. Originally applied to
labor markets (job seekers and employers), the framework applies to
any market with search frictions.

RELEVANCE TO TRADING: LOW-MODERATE
The concept of "search frictions" maps to liquidity and price
discovery in financial markets:

  - SEARCH = FINDING LIQUIDITY: In thin markets, finding the other
    side of a trade at a fair price takes time and effort. Our bot's
    limit-order-with-timeout approach (ORDER_TIMEOUT = 30s) is a
    search process with a deadline.

  - MATCHING THEORY: The DMP model suggests that match quality
    improves with patience. For our bot, being patient with limit
    orders (waiting for better fills) vs. impatient (market orders)
    involves the same tradeoff.

  - MACRO INDICATOR: Labor market search frictions (job openings vs.
    unemployment, the Beveridge curve) are leading indicators. Rising
    job openings signal economic strength; rising unemployment signals
    weakness.

POTENTIAL IMPLEMENTATION:
  - Feed JOLTS data (Job Openings and Labor Turnover Survey) into
    the LLM analyst as a macro indicator
  - Dynamically adjust ORDER_TIMEOUT based on observed fill rates
  Data needed: JOLTS data from FRED API (free)
  Complexity: Low
  Impact: Better macro context; marginal execution improvement
  Feasibility on Jetson: Trivial

2011: THOMAS SARGENT & CHRISTOPHER SIMS
"For their empirical research on cause and effect in the macroeconomy"
------------------------------------------------------------------------

CONTRIBUTION SUMMARY:
Sargent developed rational expectations econometrics, showing how
economic agents' expectations shape outcomes. Sims invented Vector
Autoregression (VAR) models, which capture the dynamic interactions
among multiple macroeconomic variables without imposing strong
theoretical assumptions.

RELEVANCE TO TRADING: MODERATE-HIGH
VAR models are directly relevant to multi-asset trading:

  - CROSS-ASSET DYNAMICS: A VAR model of our 10 crypto assets or 31
    stocks could capture lead-lag relationships. If BTC movements
    predict ETH movements 1-2 bars later, we could trade ETH before
    the move.

  - IMPULSE RESPONSES: VAR models produce impulse response functions
    showing how a shock to one variable propagates through the system.
    A shock to BTC volatility affects all crypto prices -- knowing the
    magnitude and timing helps position management.

  - MACRO VARIABLE INCLUSION: VARs can include macro variables
    (interest rates, VIX, Fear & Greed) alongside asset returns to
    model how macro shocks affect individual assets.

POTENTIAL IMPLEMENTATION:
  - Fit a small VAR model on daily returns of our crypto universe
    to detect lead-lag relationships
  - Use VAR residuals as a "surprise" indicator (actual return minus
    VAR-predicted return = genuine new information)
  - The statsmodels library supports VAR estimation
  Data needed: Historical daily returns (already available via
    market_data.py bar fetching)
  Complexity: Moderate (model fitting, feature engineering)
  Impact: Could identify cross-asset alpha; useful for timing entries
  Feasibility on Jetson: Moderate -- VAR on 10 assets is lightweight.
    statsmodels runs fine on ARM64.

2013: EUGENE FAMA, LARS PETER HANSEN & ROBERT SHILLER
"For their empirical analysis of asset prices"
------------------------------------------------------------------------

*** THIS IS THE MOST DIRECTLY RELEVANT PRIZE FOR OUR SYSTEM ***

CONTRIBUTION SUMMARY:
This prize remarkably honored both sides of the market efficiency
debate simultaneously:

  FAMA - Efficient Market Hypothesis (EMH):
    - Weak form: Past prices cannot predict future prices (challenges
      technical analysis)
    - Semi-strong: Public information is instantly reflected in prices
    - Strong: Even private information is reflected
    - Developed the Fama-French Three-Factor Model (market, size,
      value) and Five-Factor Model (adds profitability, investment)

  SHILLER - Behavioral Finance / Excess Volatility:
    - Showed stock prices are FAR more volatile than justified by
      subsequent changes in dividends
    - CAPE ratio (Cyclically Adjusted Price-Earnings): 10-year
      inflation-adjusted P/E predicts long-term returns
    - "Irrational Exuberance" -- markets overshoot due to behavioral
      factors, narrative economics

  HANSEN - Generalized Method of Moments (GMM):
    - Statistical framework for testing asset pricing models without
      fully specifying the data-generating process
    - Founded "macro-finance" field connecting macro risk to asset
      prices

RELEVANCE TO TRADING: CRITICAL

1. FAMA'S CHALLENGE TO OUR MODEL:
   The EMH directly challenges whether our LSTM model CAN work. If
   markets are efficient, technical patterns should offer no edge.
   However:
   - Fama himself acknowledges markets are "efficient enough" that
     MOST investors can't beat them, not that it's impossible.
   - Our model operates in crypto markets, which are less studied,
     less liquid, and have more retail participants -- making
     efficiency weaker.
   - The Fama-French factors (size, value, momentum, profitability,
     investment) are PERSISTENT anomalies that Fama himself
     documents, suggesting markets are not perfectly efficient.
   - Our edge likely comes from SPEED (30-second cycles), breadth
     (many simultaneous positions), and discipline (no behavioral
     biases in execution).

2. THE FAMA-FRENCH FACTOR MODEL:
   Our system currently treats each symbol independently. The
   Fama-French framework suggests we should consider FACTOR EXPOSURES:
   - Market beta: How much does each stock move with SPY?
   - Size (SMB): Small caps outperform -- are we biased toward them?
   - Value (HML): High book-to-market outperforms -- are we selecting
     for this?
   - Momentum (UMD): Recent winners continue winning -- our model
     implicitly captures this through technical features, but we
     could be explicit.
   - Profitability (RMW): Profitable firms outperform.
   - Investment (CMA): Conservative investors outperform aggressive
     ones.

3. SHILLER'S CAPE RATIO:
   The CAPE ratio is one of the best-validated long-term return
   predictors. While our bot trades short-term, CAPE provides crucial
   REGIME CONTEXT:
   - Current CAPE > 35: Historically associated with below-average
     forward returns. Our bot should be more conservative (smaller
     positions, tighter stops).
   - CAPE < 15: Historically associated with excellent forward
     returns. Be more aggressive.
   - CAPE rate of change matters too: rapidly rising CAPE signals
     momentum; rapidly falling signals panic.

4. HANSEN'S GMM FOR MODEL VALIDATION:
   GMM provides a rigorous framework for evaluating whether our
   model's predictions are genuinely capturing a risk premium vs.
   just fitting noise. We could use GMM to test whether our LSTM's
   alpha is statistically significant.

SPECIFIC IMPLEMENTATIONS:

A) SHILLER CAPE REGIME FILTER
   Data: Free API at shillerdata.com or FRED (no auth needed)
   Algorithm: Fetch monthly CAPE, compute z-score relative to 20-year
   mean. Use as a scaling factor for position sizes:
     - CAPE z-score > 1.5: Reduce position sizes by 30%
     - CAPE z-score < -1.0: Increase position sizes by 20%
   Complexity: Very low (monthly HTTP fetch + simple math)
   Impact: Moderate -- protects against buying into overvalued regimes
   Feasibility on Jetson: Trivial

B) FAMA-FRENCH FACTOR EXPOSURE ANALYSIS
   Data: Factor returns from Kenneth French's Data Library (free)
   Algorithm: Regress our portfolio returns against the five factors
   to understand where our alpha comes from. If it's all explained
   by factor exposures, we have no genuine edge.
   Complexity: Moderate (monthly regression analysis)
   Impact: Critical for understanding our edge (or lack thereof)
   Feasibility on Jetson: Easy (numpy/scipy regression)

C) MOMENTUM FACTOR AS EXPLICIT FEATURE
   Data: Already available (historical returns in training data)
   Algorithm: Add 1-month and 3-month momentum rank as features to
   our LSTM model. This explicitly captures the momentum anomaly
   that Fama-French document but don't fully explain.
   Complexity: Low (feature engineering in harvest scripts)
   Impact: Potentially significant -- momentum is one of the most
   robust anomalies in financial literature
   Feasibility on Jetson: Trivial

2016: OLIVER HART & BENGT HOLMSTROM
"For their contributions to contract theory"
------------------------------------------------------------------------

CONTRIBUTION SUMMARY:
Holmstrom formalized the principal-agent problem, showing how to
design optimal incentive contracts when the agent's actions are not
fully observable. His "informativeness principle" states that contracts
should condition pay on any variable that provides information about
the agent's effort. Hart developed incomplete contract theory, showing
how ownership/control rights should be allocated when contracts cannot
specify all contingencies.

RELEVANCE TO TRADING: LOW-MODERATE
Contract theory has subtle but important implications:

  - EXECUTIVE COMPENSATION: Holmstrom's work explains CEO pay
    structures. Stocks with heavily option-laden executive comp
    may see different behavior around earnings (risk-taking to
    hit option strike prices). This is useful qualitative context.

  - INCOMPLETE CONTRACTS = INCOMPLETE MARKETS: Hart's framework
    suggests that we can never fully contract against all risks.
    For our bot, this means we should maintain conservative position
    sizing because unknown risks (black swans) exist that our model
    cannot anticipate.

  - MORAL HAZARD IN CRYPTO: DeFi protocols involve smart contracts
    (actual contracts). Contract design quality affects token value.
    Poor contract design leads to exploits (hacks), which is
    catastrophic for token prices.

POTENTIAL IMPLEMENTATION:
  - Add executive compensation structure as a factor in the
    fundamentals.py module for stock analysis
  - Flag crypto tokens with known smart contract audit issues
  Data needed: SEC filings (executive comp), audit reports (crypto)
  Complexity: Moderate (data sourcing is the challenge)
  Impact: Marginal for stocks; potentially high for crypto safety
  Feasibility on Jetson: Moderate (API calls, LLM interpretation)

2017: RICHARD THALER
"For his contributions to behavioral economics"
------------------------------------------------------------------------

*** HIGHLY RELEVANT TO TRADING SYSTEM DESIGN ***

CONTRIBUTION SUMMARY:
Thaler's work spans several key areas:

  MENTAL ACCOUNTING: People treat money differently depending on
  its source or intended use, creating artificial categories that
  lead to suboptimal decisions. A $100 loss in a "trading account"
  feels different from a $100 loss in a "savings account" even
  though money is fungible.

  ENDOWMENT EFFECT / LOSS AVERSION: People overvalue what they
  already own, leading to holding losers too long and selling
  winners too soon (the "disposition effect").

  MARKET ANOMALIES: Thaler documented numerous persistent anomalies
  including the Winner-Loser reversal effect (De Bondt & Thaler 1985),
  calendar effects (January effect), and the equity premium puzzle.

  NUDGE THEORY / LIBERTARIAN PATERNALISM: Designing choice
  architectures that guide people toward better decisions without
  restricting freedom.

RELEVANCE TO TRADING: HIGH

1. THE DISPOSITION EFFECT AND OUR STOP-LOSS DESIGN:
   Our bot's ATR-based stop-loss system (crypto_loop.py, stock_loop.py)
   is ALREADY a behavioral correction mechanism. By enforcing
   mechanical stops, we avoid the human tendency to hold losers.
   However, we can do better:

   - ASYMMETRIC STOPS: Prospect theory (Kahneman, below) shows losses
     hurt 2x as much as equivalent gains feel good. Our current
     stop-loss and take-profit are symmetric in concept (ATR * 2.0
     stop, 3:1 R:R take-profit). We should consider whether our
     take-profit levels are too tight (cutting winners too early)
     relative to our stops (which may be too loose for losers).

   - Research by Odean (1998) and others shows the disposition effect
     is "virtually zero among algorithms." Our bot inherently avoids
     this bias -- but ONLY if we don't intervene manually.

2. CALENDAR ANOMALIES:
   Thaler documented several calendar-based patterns:
   - JANUARY EFFECT: Small-cap stocks outperform in January (tax-loss
     selling reversal). Our stock universe includes some smaller names.
   - DAY-OF-WEEK EFFECT: Mondays historically show lower returns.
   - PRE-HOLIDAY EFFECT: Markets rise before holidays.
   - TURN-OF-MONTH EFFECT: Returns are higher on the last and first
     few trading days of each month.

   Many of these anomalies have weakened since publication but some
   persist, especially in less-efficient markets (crypto).

3. THE WINNER-LOSER EFFECT:
   De Bondt & Thaler (1985) showed that past 3-5 year losers
   outperform past winners (reversal). This conflicts with short-term
   momentum (Jegadeesh & Titman 1993, 3-12 months). The implication:
   - Short-term (our model's timeframe): MOMENTUM works
   - Long-term (years): REVERSAL works
   Our model should be calibrated to the momentum horizon.

SPECIFIC IMPLEMENTATIONS:

A) CALENDAR EFFECT FILTER
   Algorithm: Add time-based features to the model or as a trading
   filter:
     - Day of week (one-hot encoded) -- already possible
     - Day of month (is it turn-of-month? first 3 or last 2 days?)
     - Month (is it January? is it September, historically worst?)
   Complexity: Very low
   Impact: Marginal but free improvement
   Feasibility on Jetson: Trivial

B) ANTI-DISPOSITION AUDIT
   Algorithm: Review our stop-loss and take-profit parameters to
   ensure we're not cutting winners too early. Analyze historical
   trades to measure:
     - Average holding time for winners vs. losers
     - Average return of trades that hit take-profit vs. those that
       were sold at model signal
     - Whether widening take-profit levels improves returns
   Complexity: Low (backtesting analysis)
   Impact: Potentially significant
   Feasibility on Jetson: Easy

C) MENTAL ACCOUNTING PROTECTION
   Design principle: Our bot treats all positions as part of ONE
   portfolio, not separate mental accounts. This is correct. Ensure
   we never implement logic that treats crypto and stock positions
   differently for risk purposes when they should be viewed as a
   unified portfolio.

2019: ABHIJIT BANERJEE, ESTHER DUFLO & MICHAEL KREMER
"For their experimental approach to alleviating global poverty"
------------------------------------------------------------------------

CONTRIBUTION SUMMARY:
Pioneered the use of Randomized Controlled Trials (RCTs) in
development economics. By randomly assigning "treatment" and "control"
groups, they could establish causal effects of specific interventions
on poverty reduction.

RELEVANCE TO TRADING: MODERATE (METHODOLOGY)
Their RCT methodology is directly applicable as a FRAMEWORK for
evaluating our trading system:

  - A/B TESTING OUR STRATEGIES: We should treat parameter changes
    as experiments. Run the bot with parameter set A on some symbols
    and parameter set B on others, measuring outcomes. This is
    exactly the RCT approach.

  - AVOIDING DATA SNOOPING: The RCT framework emphasizes
    pre-registration of hypotheses to avoid p-hacking. When we
    optimize our LSTM hyperparameters (hypersearch_v2.py), we should
    use walk-forward validation (which we already do) and
    out-of-sample testing to avoid overfitting.

  - CAUSAL INFERENCE VS. CORRELATION: Our LSTM may find patterns
    that are correlational, not causal. The RCT mindset encourages
    asking "does this feature CAUSE better predictions, or is it
    just correlated with something that does?"

POTENTIAL IMPLEMENTATION:
  - Formalize A/B testing framework: split symbols into test/control
    groups when testing new parameters
  - Add out-of-sample reporting to hypersearch output
  Complexity: Moderate
  Impact: Better parameter selection, less overfitting
  Feasibility on Jetson: Easy

2020: PAUL MILGROM & ROBERT WILSON
"For improvements to auction theory and inventions of new auction formats"
------------------------------------------------------------------------

CONTRIBUTION SUMMARY:
Wilson developed theory for auctions with "common values" (where the
item has the same value to all bidders, but each bidder has imperfect
information about that value). He formalized the "winner's curse" --
the winning bidder tends to have the most optimistic estimate, so
winning is actually bad news about the item's true value.

Milgrom generalized auction theory to handle both common values and
private values, showing how auction format design affects outcomes.

RELEVANCE TO TRADING: MODERATE-HIGH

1. THE WINNER'S CURSE IN TRADING:
   When our bot buys a stock, it's "winning an auction" -- the
   current ask price represents the cheapest someone is willing to
   sell. If everyone else is selling, maybe they know something we
   don't. This is the adverse selection problem.

   For our system specifically:
   - When our LSTM model gives a strong BUY signal but the price has
     ALREADY moved up significantly, we may be buying at the top
     (winner's curse). The signal is strongest when prices have
     risen, but that's also when we're most likely overpaying.
   - SOLUTION: Incorporate a "contrarian discount" -- when the model
     is bullish AND price has already moved substantially (e.g.,
     above its 20-period SMA by > 2 ATRs), reduce position size
     or increase conviction threshold.

2. INFORMATION AGGREGATION:
   Milgrom showed that auction prices aggregate dispersed information.

2002: DANIEL KAHNEMAN (with Vernon Smith)
"For having integrated insights from psychological research into

------------------------------------------------------------------------

*** FOUNDATIONAL TO OUR SYSTEM'S DESIGN PHILOSOPHY ***

CONTRIBUTION SUMMARY:
Kahneman and Amos Tversky (who died before the prize) developed
Prospect Theory, which describes how people ACTUALLY make decisions
under uncertainty (as opposed to how rational agents SHOULD):

  LOSS AVERSION: Losses hurt approximately 2x as much as equivalent
  gains feel good. A $100 loss feels as bad as a $200 gain feels
  good.

  REFERENCE DEPENDENCE: People evaluate outcomes relative to a
  reference point (usually the status quo), not in absolute terms.

  PROBABILITY WEIGHTING: People overweight small probabilities
  (why they buy lottery tickets and insurance) and underweight large
  probabilities.

  FRAMING EFFECTS: How a choice is presented affects the decision.
  "90% survival rate" vs. "10% mortality rate" elicit different
  choices despite being mathematically identical.

  ANCHORING: Initial information disproportionately influences
  subsequent judgments.

RELEVANCE TO TRADING: CRITICAL

1. LOSS AVERSION AND STOP-LOSS DESIGN:
   Our stop-loss system should be designed with Kahneman's insights:
   - Humans hold losers too long (loss aversion -> reluctance to
     realize losses). Our mechanical stops prevent this.
   - Humans sell winners too soon (risk aversion for gains). Our
     trailing stop system partially addresses this, but we should
     verify our take-profit levels aren't too tight.

2. THE DISPOSITION EFFECT:
   Kahneman's prospect theory explains the disposition effect
   (Thaler, above). Our bot is IMMUNE to this because it follows
   mechanical rules. This is a genuine EDGE over human traders.
   We should NEVER add manual override capability.

3. MARKET PARTICIPANT BEHAVIOR:
   OTHER market participants (especially retail crypto traders) DO
   exhibit these biases. We can EXPLOIT them:
   - ANCHORING at round numbers (BTC at $50K, $60K, $100K) creates
     support/resistance levels that are behaviorally driven
   - OVERREACTION to bad news (loss aversion amplifies selling
     pressure) creates buying opportunities after negative events
   - UNDERREACTION to gradual changes (probability weighting) means
     slow trends are often underpriced

4. PROBABILITY CALIBRATION:
   Kahneman showed people are terrible at estimating probabilities.
   Our LSTM model outputs predicted returns, not probabilities. If
   we convert to probabilities (e.g., probability of positive return),
   we should NOT apply human probability intuitions to them. Use
   the raw model output with proper calibration.

2003: ROBERT ENGLE (with Clive Granger)
"For methods of analyzing economic time series with time-varying
volatility (ARCH)"
------------------------------------------------------------------------

*** MOST DIRECTLY IMPLEMENTABLE IMPROVEMENT FOR OUR SYSTEM ***

CONTRIBUTION SUMMARY:
Engle developed ARCH (Autoregressive Conditional Heteroskedasticity)
models in 1982. His student Tim Bollerslev generalized this to GARCH
(1986), the most widely used volatility model in finance.

Key insight: VOLATILITY CLUSTERS. Periods of high volatility tend to
be followed by more high volatility, and periods of low volatility
tend to continue. GARCH models capture this pattern by making today's
volatility forecast depend on both recent squared returns (ARCH
component) AND the previous volatility forecast (GARCH component).

The GARCH(1,1) model:
  sigma_t^2 = omega + alpha * r_{t-1}^2 + beta * sigma_{t-1}^2

Where:
  sigma_t^2 = today's variance forecast
  omega = long-run variance constant
  alpha = weight on yesterday's squared return (ARCH term)
  beta = weight on yesterday's variance forecast (GARCH term)

RELEVANCE TO TRADING: CRITICAL

Our current volatility measure is ATR (Average True Range), which is
a simple rolling average of true ranges. GARCH offers several
advantages:

1. ATR vs. GARCH COMPARISON:
   ATR:
   + Simple, robust, no estimation required
   + Well-understood by practitioners
   - Equal weighting of all observations in window
   - Doesn't capture volatility clustering directly
   - Slow to react to regime changes (uses 14-period average)
   - No forward-looking forecast capability

   GARCH:
   + Captures volatility clustering (the most important volatility
     feature)
   + Exponentially decays old information (recent data matters more)
   + Provides genuine FORECASTS of future volatility
   + Can model asymmetric response (bad news -> more volatility,
     via GJR-GARCH)
   + Allows computation of Value-at-Risk (VaR)
   - Requires parameter estimation
   - More computationally expensive
   - Can be numerically unstable with short series

2. GARCH FOR STOP-LOSS IMPROVEMENT:
   Our current stop-loss: entry - (ATR * multiplier)
   GARCH-based stop: entry - (GARCH_forecast * multiplier)

   The key advantage: GARCH FORECASTS adapt faster to changing
   volatility regimes. When volatility suddenly spikes (e.g., news
   event), GARCH immediately widens the expected range, while ATR
   takes several periods to catch up. This means:
   - Fewer premature stop-outs during volatility spikes
   - Tighter stops during calm periods (capturing smaller moves)

3. GARCH FOR POSITION SIZING:
   Kelly-optimal position sizing depends on expected return / variance.
   If we have better variance forecasts (GARCH), we get better
   position sizing:
   - Low GARCH forecast -> larger positions (calm market)
   - High GARCH forecast -> smaller positions (volatile market)

4. HYBRID LSTM-GARCH:
   Recent research (2024-2025) shows that LSTM-GARCH hybrid models
   outperform either alone for volatility forecasting in crypto
   portfolios. Our existing LSTM architecture could incorporate
   GARCH residuals as features:
   - GARCH standardized residual = return / GARCH_sigma
   - If |standardized residual| > 3, it's an outlier event
   - The standardized residual series has nicer statistical properties
     than raw returns

SPECIFIC IMPLEMENTATIONS:

A) GARCH(1,1) VOLATILITY FORECASTING MODULE
   Library: arch (pip install arch) -- confirmed ARM64/aarch64
   compatible with pre-built wheels
   Algorithm:
     1. Fit GARCH(1,1) on 100-250 recent daily returns
     2. Forecast 1-day-ahead volatility
     3. Use as replacement or supplement to ATR in stop-loss calc
   Code sketch:
     from arch import arch_model
     am = arch_model(returns, vol='Garch', p=1, q=1, dist='t')
     res = am.fit(disp='off')
     forecast = res.forecast(horizon=1)
     sigma_1d = np.sqrt(forecast.variance.values[-1, 0])
   Complexity: Moderate (fitting GARCH, handling edge cases)
   Impact: SIGNIFICANT -- better volatility forecasts improve
   stop-loss placement, position sizing, and risk management
   Feasibility on Jetson: GOOD -- arch package supports aarch64.
   GARCH fitting on 250 observations takes < 1 second. Could fit
   once per hour, not every 30-second cycle.

B) GJR-GARCH FOR ASYMMETRIC VOLATILITY
   Enhancement: GJR-GARCH adds a term for negative returns (leverage
   effect). In stocks, bad news increases volatility more than good
   news by the same magnitude. In crypto, the asymmetry is even
   stronger.
   Algorithm: Same as above but vol='GARCH' -> vol='GJR-GARCH'
   Complexity: Same as GARCH(1,1)
   Impact: Slightly better than standard GARCH for stocks
   Feasibility on Jetson: Same as above

C) GARCH-BASED POSITION SIZING
   Algorithm: Replace fixed NOTIONAL_PER_SYMBOL with:
     adjusted_notional = BASE_NOTIONAL * (TARGET_VOL / garch_sigma)
   Where TARGET_VOL is our desired per-position volatility (e.g., 2%).
   This ensures each position contributes roughly equal risk.
   Complexity: Low (once GARCH is implemented)
   Impact: SIGNIFICANT -- risk parity across positions
   Feasibility on Jetson: Trivial

D) GARCH FEATURES FOR LSTM
   Algorithm: Add to the feature set in indicators.py:
     - GARCH conditional volatility (sigma_t)
     - GARCH standardized residuals (r_t / sigma_t)
     - GARCH volatility forecast ratio (sigma_{t+1} / sigma_t)
   Complexity: Moderate (integrate GARCH into harvest pipeline)
   Impact: Potentially significant -- gives the LSTM better
   volatility information than raw ATR
   Feasibility on Jetson: Good (GARCH fitting in batch during harvest)

================================================================================
PART IV: CROSS-CUTTING THEMES AND SYNTHESIS
================================================================================

------------------------------------------------------------------------
THEME 1: THE EFFICIENCY-BEHAVIORAL FINANCE SPECTRUM
------------------------------------------------------------------------

The 2013 prize (Fama + Shiller) represents the central tension in
our system. Our bot implicitly assumes:
  - Markets are NOT perfectly efficient (otherwise our LSTM has no
    edge)
  - Markets are MOSTLY efficient (otherwise everyone would be rich)
  - Our edge comes from exploiting the gap between efficiency and
    behavioral irrationality

The practical synthesis:
  - In LIQUID, WELL-STUDIED MARKETS (S&P 500 stocks), our edge is
    small. Focus on risk management over alpha generation.
  - In LESS EFFICIENT MARKETS (crypto, small caps), behavioral
    anomalies are larger and our model may have a genuine edge.
  - Our BIGGEST EDGE is not prediction accuracy but DISCIPLINE:
    mechanical execution without behavioral biases (disposition
    effect, loss aversion, anchoring).

------------------------------------------------------------------------
THEME 2: VOLATILITY IS THE KEY STATE VARIABLE
------------------------------------------------------------------------

Multiple laureates' work converges on volatility as the single most
important variable for risk management:
  - Engle (2003): GARCH for volatility forecasting
  - Merton/Scholes (1997): Implied volatility from options
  - Markowitz (1990): Variance in portfolio optimization
  - Hansen (2013): Volatility in stochastic discount factors

Our current ATR-based approach is a reasonable approximation, but
GARCH offers a theoretically superior and empirically validated
improvement. Priority recommendation: Implement GARCH.

------------------------------------------------------------------------
THEME 3: INFORMATION IS EVERYTHING
------------------------------------------------------------------------

Several prizes emphasize information processing:
  - Akerlof/Spence/Stiglitz (2001): Information asymmetry
  - Milgrom/Wilson (2020): Information in auctions
  - Kahneman (2002): Biased information processing

Our system already has a two-layer information architecture:
  - QUANTITATIVE: LSTM model processes technical features
  - QUALITATIVE: LLM analyst processes news, fundamentals, macro

This dual-layer approach is well-aligned with the academic literature.
The key improvement would be making the QUANTITATIVE layer aware of
VOLATILITY STATE (GARCH) and making the QUALITATIVE layer aware of
MACRO STATE (financial stress indices, CAPE, VIX, inflation
expectations).

------------------------------------------------------------------------
THEME 4: TAIL RISK AND SYSTEMIC EVENTS
------------------------------------------------------------------------

Bernanke/Diamond/Dybvig (2022) remind us that the most damaging
events are systemic: bank runs, exchange collapses, liquidity crises.
Our bot needs protection against these fat-tail events:

Current protection:
  + Circuit breaker (5% daily equity drawdown -> flatten)
  + Individual stop-losses (ATR-based)
  + Sentiment gating (Fear & Greed Index)

Missing protection:
  - Financial stress index monitoring
  - Stablecoin peg monitoring (crypto-specific)
  - VIX regime awareness
  - Cross-asset correlation spike detection (all correlations -> 1
    during crises)

------------------------------------------------------------------------
THEME 5: THE BEHAVIORAL EDGE
------------------------------------------------------------------------

Kahneman (2002) and Thaler (2017) provide our bot's most fundamental
advantage: it doesn't have human psychology. While other market
participants (especially retail traders in crypto) exhibit:
  - Loss aversion -> hold losers too long
  - Disposition effect -> sell winners too early
  - Anchoring -> fixate on entry prices
  - Overconfidence -> over-trade
  - Herding -> buy at tops, sell at bottoms

Our bot follows mechanical rules. This is an edge that persists
because it's rooted in human psychology, not in market structure.
As long as markets have human participants, behavioral biases create
exploitable inefficiencies.

------------------------------------------------------------------------
THEME 6: MACRO-FINANCE INTEGRATION
------------------------------------------------------------------------

Several prizes emphasize the link between macroeconomics and asset
prices: Phelps (2006), Sargent/Sims (2011), Bernanke (2022). Our
current system has limited macro awareness (Fear & Greed Index,
LLM macro commentary). The recommended enhancement path:

Level 1 (current): Fear & Greed Index + LLM macro assessment
Level 2 (recommended): Add VIX, financial stress indices, CAPE
Level 3 (aspirational): Add TIPS breakeven rates, yield curve
  slope, credit spreads, JOLTS data

Each level adds macro regime awareness at minimal computational cost
(HTTP requests to free APIs).

------------------------------------------------------------------------
HARDWARE FEASIBILITY SUMMARY (Jetson Orin Nano, 8GB)
------------------------------------------------------------------------

All recommended implementations are feasible on the Jetson:

  - GARCH fitting (arch package): Confirmed ARM64 compatible. Fitting
    on 250 data points takes < 1 second. Fit once per hour, not
    every 30s cycle. Memory: < 50MB additional.

  - Correlation matrix (numpy): 10x10 or 31x31 covariance matrix is
    trivial. < 1ms computation. < 1MB memory.

  - HMM regime detection (hmmlearn): Confirmed ARM64 compatible.
    Fitting a 3-state HMM on 250 daily returns takes < 1 second.
    < 20MB memory.

  - PyPortfolioOpt / scipy optimization: Pure Python + scipy, works
    on ARM64. Optimization for 10-31 assets takes < 100ms. < 20MB
    memory.

  - API calls (FRED, Shiller, VIX): HTTP requests, negligible compute.
    < 1MB memory each.

  Total additional memory overhead: < 100MB, well within our 8GB budget.
  All operations can be batched (run once per hour or once per day)
  rather than every 30-second trading cycle, so there is no impact
  on cycle time.

1. IMPLEMENT GARCH VOLATILITY FORECASTING (Engle 2003)
   Replace or augment ATR with GARCH for stop-losses and position
   sizing. The arch Python package runs on ARM64. This is the single

   perspective.

2. ADD FINANCIAL STRESS / VIX REGIME FILTERS (Bernanke 2022 +
   Merton/Scholes 1997)
   Monitor systemic risk indicators (STLFSI2, VIX) and scale down
   exposure during stress periods. Free data from FRED and yfinance.
   This protects against the tail-risk events our LSTM model cannot
   predict.

3. CORRELATION-AWARE POSITION SIZING (Markowitz 1990)
   Stop treating each position independently. Compute rolling
   correlations and ensure the portfolio has genuine diversification.
   This is the biggest gap in our current system's risk management.

4. LEVERAGE OUR BEHAVIORAL EDGE (Kahneman 2002 + Thaler 2017)
   Our mechanical execution is an edge over human traders. Verify
   this edge is maintained (audit for disposition effect, validate
   stop-loss asymmetry). Never add manual override capabilities.
   Consider exploiting others' behavioral biases (overreaction
   detector, round-number support/resistance).

5. MACRO REGIME AWARENESS LAYER (Shiller 2013 + Phelps 2006)
   Add CAPE ratio, inflation expectations, and economic calendar
   awareness to the LLM analyst's macro context. This gives the
   qualitative layer better regime information for adjusting
   conviction scores.
