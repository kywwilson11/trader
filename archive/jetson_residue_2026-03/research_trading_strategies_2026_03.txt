1. ML-BASED TRADING: ATTENTION/TRANSFORMER vs LSTM
================================================================================

KEY FINDING: Transformers outperform standalone LSTMs, but hybrid architectures
outperform both. The Temporal Fusion Transformer (TFT) is the current
state-of-the-art for financial time series.

SOURCES:
- "Hybrid CNN-LSTM Integrated with TFT for Stock Market Forecasting" (IIETA 2024)
  https://www.iieta.org/journals/isi/paper/10.18280/isi.301122
- "Temporal Fusion Transformers for Enhanced Multivariate Time Series" (IJCSIT 2024)
  https://thesai.org/Downloads/Volume15No7/Paper_13-Temporal_Fusion_Transformers_for_Enhanced_Multivariate_Time_Series.pdf
- "Adaptive Temporal Fusion Transformers for Cryptocurrency Prediction" (arXiv 2025)
  https://arxiv.org/html/2509.10542v1
- "Predictive Modeling of Stock Prices Using Transformer Model" (ACM ICMLT 2024)
  https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3674029.3674037
- "Lamformer: LSTM-enhanced Agent Attention and MoE Transformer" (Springer 2025)
  https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s13042-025-02740-8
- "CNN-Trans-SPP: Small Transformer with CNN" (ERA 2024)
  https://aimspress.com/article/doi/10.3934/era.2024314?viewType=HTML
- "Revisiting Ensemble Methods for Trading" (arXiv 2501.10709, FinRL 2023/2024)
  https://arxiv.org/html/2501.10709v1
- "TFT-Based Trading Strategy for Multi-Crypto Assets" (MDPI Systems 2025)
  https://www.mdpi.com/2079-8954/13/6/474

PERFORMANCE DATA:
- Pure Transformer vs LSTM: Transformer achieved MAE 0.0826 vs LSTM's 0.2890 (3.5x better)
- CNN-Transformer hybrid: reduced error by 45% vs LSTM, 32% vs attention-LSTM, 37% vs
  pure Transformer
- TFT vs LSTM: 40-50% reduction in MAE, MAPE below 2% across all stocks tested
- TFT in bear markets: MAE 0.18 vs 0.22 (Vanilla Transformer) vs 0.25 (LSTM)
- TFT trading strategy: +807% return (Jan 2024 - Sep 2025) vs +38% buy-and-hold
- Performance gap widens for longer prediction horizons, suggesting TFT captures
  long-term dependencies better

WHY TFT IS SUPERIOR:
- Variable Selection Network: automatically identifies which inputs matter
- Multi-horizon attention: captures patterns at different time scales
- Static covariate encoding: incorporates non-time-varying features (sector, asset class)
- Interpretability: attention weights show WHICH features drove predictions and WHEN
- Combines Transformer self-attention with LSTM seq2seq approach

LIGHTWEIGHT ALTERNATIVES FOR EDGE DEVICES (Jetson Orin Nano):
- LAMFormer: Uses Agent Attention (reduces computational burden of full self-attention)
  with Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) for adaptive multi-scale temporal features
- CNN-Trans-SPP: Small transformer with CNN feature extraction, designed for
  resource-constrained environments
- Attention-augmented LSTM: Add attention layer on top of existing LSTM rather than
  full transformer replacement

ACTION ITEMS FOR THE BOT:
(a) The bot already has MultiheadAttention on LSTM (model_v2.py) -- this is good
(b) Consider TFT migration for next major version (PyTorch Forecasting library)
(c) For Jetson Orin Nano: LAMFormer or attention-augmented LSTM more practical than
    full TFT due to 8GB memory constraint
(d) Use TFT's Variable Selection Network concept to auto-select best features from
    the current feature set
(e) Explore Adaptive TFT for crypto specifically (arXiv 2509.10542)

2. POSITION SIZING BEYOND KELLY
================================================================================

KEY FINDING: Hybrid Kelly-VIX sizing outperforms pure Kelly or pure volatility
targeting. Half-Kelly is the practical sweet spot for most traders.

SOURCES:
- "Sizing the Risk: Kelly, VIX, and Hybrid Approaches" (arXiv 2508.16598, Aug 2025)
  https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.16598
- "Kelly Criterion vs Fixed Fractional" (Medium, Jan 2026)
  https://medium.com/@tmapendembe_28659/kelly-criterion-vs-fixed-fractional-which-risk-model-maximizes-long-term-growth-972ecb606e6c
- "Risk Before Returns: Position Sizing Frameworks" (Medium 2025)
  https://medium.com/@ildiveliu/risk-before-returns-position-sizing-frameworks-fixed-fractional-atr-based-kelly-lite-4513f770a82a
- "Kelly Criterion Position Sizing" (Astute Investors Calculus)
  https://astuteinvestorscalculus.com/kelly-criterion-position-sizing/

KEY INSIGHTS:

A. HYBRID KELLY-VIX SIZING (NEW - arXiv 2508.16598, 2025):
   - Combines Kelly's optimal growth with VIX regime awareness
   - Consistently balances return generation with drawdown control
   - Particularly effective in low-volatility regimes (e.g., 2024)
   - Outperforms pure Kelly during regime transitions
   - Tested on S&P 500 options strategies with rigorous methodology

B. FRACTIONAL KELLY RECOMMENDATIONS:
   - Full Kelly: Maximum growth rate but extreme drawdowns (50%+ common)
   - Half Kelly (f/2): 75% of full Kelly return, 50% of the variance
   - Third Kelly (f/3): Still 55% of return, 33% of variance
   - Quarter Kelly (f/4): Recommended for high estimation uncertainty
   - Key insight: Kelly is extremely sensitive to edge estimation error.
     If your estimated edge is off by even 20%, full Kelly will LOSE money

C. VOLATILITY-TARGETING COMPARISON:
   - ATR-based sizing: normalizes risk per trade by volatility
   - GARCH-based (current bot approach): forward-looking vol estimate
   - Realized vol targeting: scale positions to target constant portfolio vol
   - All three reduce drawdowns vs fixed-size positions
   - GARCH + fractional Kelly hybrid likely optimal for the bot

D. FIXED FRACTIONAL (Vince 1990):
   - More stable equity curve, lower volatility
   - Mathematically prevents ruin (position size shrinks with losses)
   - Lower ceiling on long-term growth vs Kelly

ACTION ITEMS FOR THE BOT:
(a) Implement hybrid Kelly-VIX: scale Kelly fraction by VIX regime
    - Low VIX (<15): use 0.5 Kelly
    - Medium VIX (15-25): use 0.35 Kelly
    - High VIX (25-35): use 0.25 Kelly
    - Crisis VIX (>35): use 0.15 Kelly
(b) Add estimation error buffer: multiply Kelly fraction by (1 - uncertainty)
    where uncertainty comes from prediction confidence interval
(c) The bot already has GARCH vol targeting -- keep it, combine with Kelly-VIX
(d) Consider "anti-martingale" scaling: increase size after wins, decrease
    after losses (aligns with Kelly mathematics)

3. REGIME DETECTION IMPROVEMENTS
================================================================================

KEY FINDING: Online Bayesian change-point detection outperforms fixed-state HMM
for real-time regime detection. Ensemble HMM + ML voting frameworks show
strongest results.

SOURCES:
- "A Forest of Opinions: Multi-Model Ensemble-HMM Voting Framework" (AIMS 2025)
  https://www.aimspress.com/article/id/69045d2fba35de34708adb5d
- "Online Learning of Order Flow with Bayesian Change-Point Detection"
  (Quantitative Finance, 2024)
  https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/14697688.2024.2337300
- "Bayesian Autoregressive Online Change-Point Detection" (arXiv 2024)
  https://arxiv.org/html/2407.16376v1
- "Downside Risk Reduction Using Regime-Switching Signals: Statistical Jump Model"
  (arXiv 2024)
  https://arxiv.org/html/2402.05272v2

KEY INSIGHTS:

A. ONLINE BAYESIAN CHANGE-POINT DETECTION (BOCPD):
   - Does NOT require pre-specifying number of regimes (unlike current HMM)
   - Updates beliefs in real-time as each new data point arrives
   - Score-driven approach accommodates temporal correlations within regimes
   - Allows variance and correlation to vary within each regime
   - Superior to traditional HMM for detecting novel market regimes

B. ENSEMBLE HMM + ML VOTING (2025):
   - Dual pipeline architecture:
     Pipeline 1: Ensemble ML (bagging + boosting) on technical indicators
     Pipeline 2: HMM on macroeconomic + long-horizon technical indicators
   - Voting classifier combines both pipelines for robust regime classification
   - Incorporates comprehensive macro + technical indicators for holistic view
   - Better than either approach alone

C. STATISTICAL JUMP MODEL (2024):
   - Adds "jump penalty" at state transitions (encourages regime persistence)
   - Reduces false regime switches that plague standard HMM
   - Specifically designed for downside risk reduction
   - Demonstrated reduction in maximum drawdown

D. BIC-BASED MODEL SELECTION:
   - Fit models with 3 to 9 states
   - Select model with lowest Bayesian Information Criterion
   - Current bot may be using suboptimal number of regimes

ACTION ITEMS FOR THE BOT:
(a) Add BOCPD as complementary signal to existing HMM
    - Python package: bayesian_changepoint_detection (pip installable)
    - Run in parallel with HMM, use agreement as confidence signal
(b) Implement jump penalty in HMM transitions to reduce false switches
(c) Add BIC-based regime count selection to hypersearch
(d) Consider ensemble voting: combine HMM regime + GARCH regime + macro regime
    for final regime classification (the bot already has all three signals)
(e) Add online parameter updating: re-estimate HMM parameters on rolling window

4. TRANSACTION COST MODELING IN BACKTESTS
================================================================================

KEY FINDING: Most backtests dramatically overfit by assuming zero or minimal
transaction costs. Realistic cost modeling typically reduces strategy returns
by 50-80%. Proper walk-forward validation with costs produces Sharpe ~0.3-0.5,
not the 2.0+ commonly reported.

SOURCES:
- "Interpretable Hypothesis-Driven Trading: Rigorous Walk-Forward Validation"
  (arXiv 2512.12924, Dec 2025)
  https://arxiv.org/html/2512.12924v1
- "Impact of Transaction Costs and Slippage on Algorithmic Trading Performance"
  (ResearchGate 2024)
  https://www.researchgate.net/publication/384458498
- "TCA: Benchmarks and Slippage" (Talos 2024)
  https://www.talos.com/insights/execution-insights-through-transaction-cost-analysis-tca-benchmarks-and-slippage
- "Backtest Overfitting in the ML Era" (ScienceDirect 2024)
  https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0950705124011110
- "Combinatorial Purged Cross-Validation" (various 2024-2025)
  https://www.quantbeckman.com/p/with-code-combinatorial-purged-cross

KEY INSIGHTS:

A. REALISTIC COST MODEL (from arXiv 2512.12924):
   - Fixed costs: $1 per trade (commission)
   - Variable costs: 5 basis points (0.05%) x position size x execution price
   - Orders execute at NEXT DAY'S OPEN (not signal-time close)
   - Position constraints: max 5 concurrent, 20% per position, 50% per sector
   - Result: 0.55% annualized return, Sharpe 0.33, max drawdown -2.76%
   - This is HONEST. Most published strategies claiming Sharpe >1.5 are overfit

B. COST COMPONENTS TO MODEL:
   1. Commission/fees: Alpaca = $0 equity, varies for crypto
   2. Spread cost: half the bid-ask spread per trade
   3. Market impact: price movement caused by your order
   4. Slippage: difference between signal price and execution price
   5. Opportunity cost: orders that don't fill
   - Quadratic models best capture market impact (nonlinear with size)

C. COMBINATORIAL PURGED CROSS-VALIDATION (CPCV):
   - Lopez de Prado's method for detecting backtest overfitting
   - Systematically constructs multiple train-test splits
   - Purges overlapping samples, enforces embargo period
   - 2024 study found CPCV "markedly superior" in detecting overfitting
   - Computes Probability of Backtest Overfitting (PBO)
   - If PBO > 0.5, strategy is likely overfit

D. WALK-FORWARD BEST PRACTICES (from 2025 framework):
   - Use 34+ rolling windows over 10+ year period
   - 252-day training, 63-day testing per window
   - Strict information set discipline (no future data)
   - Must succeed across MULTIPLE independent test periods
   - Report p-values honestly (most strategies p > 0.1)

ACTION ITEMS FOR THE BOT:
(a) Add spread-cost tracking to backtest: measure actual bid-ask at signal time
(b) Execute backtest trades at next bar's open, not signal bar's close
(c) Add market impact model: for positions >1% of ADV, add sqrt(size/ADV) * spread
(d) Implement PBO calculation in hypersearch_v2.py to detect overfit parameters
(e) Add embargo period between train/test sets (at least 5 bars)
(f) Track and report realistic Sharpe ratios (after all costs)

5. STOP LOSS RESEARCH
================================================================================

KEY FINDING: Trailing stops at 15-20% DO improve risk-adjusted returns vs
buy-and-hold. Very tight stops (5%) HURT returns. ATR-based or volatility-
adjusted stops outperform fixed-percentage stops. DRL-based dynamic stops
are an emerging frontier.

SOURCES:
- "Effectiveness of Trailing Stop-Loss Strategies" (Portfolio Management Research)
  https://www.pm-research.com/content/iijindinv/14/1/29
- "Risk Reduction Using Trailing Stop-Loss Rules" (Dai et al., Int Review Finance 2021)
  https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/Delivery.cfm/SSRN_ID3338243_code3381625.pdf
- "Trailing Stop-Loss and Re-Entry Strategies in Europe" (EJBMR)
  https://www.ejbmr.org/index.php/ejbmr/article/view/1426
- "Dynamic Stop Loss with Deep Reinforcement Learning on NASDAQ" (Springer 2024)
  https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-031-78465-1_15
- "Optimal Trading with a Trailing Stop" (arXiv 1701.03960)
  https://arxiv.org/abs/1701.03960

KEY INSIGHTS:

A. OPTIMAL STOP-LOSS LEVELS:
   - Highest quarterly return: 20% trailing stop (1.71% average)
   - Highest cumulative return: 15% trailing stop (73.91% over study period)
   - 5% trailing stop: WORSE than buy-and-hold (excessive whipsawing)
   - Sweet spot: 1.0-1.5 standard deviations from peak
   - Trailing stops outperformed traditional stops by 27.47% over 11 years at
     the 20% level

B. ATR-BASED STOPS (PREFERRED):
   - Set stop at price - N * ATR (where N typically 2-3)
   - Automatically widens in volatile markets, tightens in calm markets
   - Avoids whipsaw in volatile stocks, maintains protection in calm ones
   - The bot already calculates ATR -- easy to implement

C. VOLATILITY-ADJUSTED STOPS:
   - Use GARCH-forecasted volatility to set stop distance
   - Wider stops when volatility is expected to increase
   - Tighter stops when volatility is expected to decrease
   - Synergy with existing GARCH module

D. DRL-BASED DYNAMIC STOPS (EMERGING):
   - Deep Reinforcement Learning agent learns optimal stop levels
   - Adapts to changing market conditions in real-time
   - Tested on NASDAQ-100 stocks (2014-2024)
   - Three-component architecture: trend detection + price prediction + stop
   - Promising but computationally expensive for edge deployment

E. RE-ENTRY AFTER STOP:
   - Critical: re-entry strategy matters as much as stop placement
   - Re-enter when price recovers above a moving average or breaks prior high
   - Without re-entry rules, stops just lock in losses

ACTION ITEMS FOR THE BOT:
(a) Implement ATR-based trailing stops: stop = peak_price - 2.5 * ATR
    - The bot already has ATR calculation in market_data.py
    - 2.5 ATR provides ~15-20% trailing distance for typical stocks
(b) Add GARCH-adjusted stop widening: multiply ATR factor by
    garch_vol / historical_vol ratio
(c) Implement re-entry logic: if stopped out, re-enter when price crosses
    above 10-day SMA
(d) Track stop-loss hit rate and adjust ATR multiplier via Optuna
(e) Do NOT use tight stops (< 1 ATR) -- research is clear they hurt returns

6. ENSEMBLE METHODS FOR TRADING SIGNALS
================================================================================

KEY FINDING: Combining diverse models via weighted averaging or voting
consistently reduces drawdown and improves risk-adjusted returns. The key is
model DIVERSITY, not model count. Raw price features outperform technical
indicators for ML prediction accuracy.

SOURCES:
- "Revisiting Ensemble Methods for Stock/Crypto Trading" (arXiv 2501.10709, 2025)
  https://arxiv.org/html/2501.10709v1
- "Assessing Impact of Technical Indicators on ML Stock Prediction" (arXiv 2412.15448)
  https://arxiv.org/html/2412.15448v1
- "Combined Momentum and Mean Reversion Strategy" (Multiple sources)
- "Multi-Agent Stock Prediction Systems" (arXiv 2502.15853, 2025)
  https://arxiv.org/html/2502.15853v1

KEY INSIGHTS:

A. ENSEMBLE PERFORMANCE (FinRL Contests 2023/2024):
   - Stock ensemble (PPO+SAC+DDPG): 46.89-62.60% returns vs 18.95% market baseline
   - Maximum drawdown reduction: up to 4.17% vs individual agents
   - Sharpe ratio improvement: up to +0.21 over component models
   - Crypto ensemble (DQN+DDQN+Dueling): max drawdown -0.73% vs -0.98% individual
   - Win/loss ratio: 1.62 ensemble vs 1.31 individual

B. CRITICAL INFRASTRUCTURE INSIGHT:
   - Massively parallel simulation on GPU: 2,048 environments
   - 1,746x speedup for crypto, 47.73x for stocks
   - KL divergence regularization forces model diversity
   - Varied datasets (with +/-1% price adjustments) prevent overfitting

C. TECHNICAL INDICATOR FINDINGS (IMPORTANT WARNING):
   - RAW PRICE FEATURES (close, open, high) consistently OUTPERFORMED technical
     indicators for prediction accuracy
   - RSI and Stochastic Oscillator showed "inconsistent or minimal impacts"
   - Bollinger Bands + hybrid combos: better for RISK MANAGEMENT, not prediction
   - All models showed NEGATIVE out-of-sample R-squared (overfitting warning)
   - Implication: don't over-rely on technical indicators for ML prediction

D. MOMENTUM + MEAN-REVERSION COMBINATION:
   - Prioritize entries where momentum AND mean-reversion signals agree
   - Use momentum indicators to avoid trading against strong trends
   - Mean-reversion signals strongest in range-bound/low-vol regimes
   - Regime detection (HMM) should gate which signal type gets priority
   - Risk-adjusted position sizing manages exposure at signal level

E. MULTI-SYSTEM CONFIRMATION:
   - Require confirmation from 2+ systems to generate final trade signal
   - Dramatically reduces false signals

ACTION ITEMS FOR THE BOT:
(a) The bot already has LSTM + LightGBM ensemble -- add signal agreement weighting
(b) Consider testing raw OHLCV as primary ML features, with indicators as
    secondary confirmation (this contradicts common wisdom but evidence supports it)
(c) Add regime-gated signal selection:
    - Trending regime (Hurst > 0.6): weight momentum signals higher
    - Mean-reverting regime (Hurst < 0.4): weight mean-reversion signals higher
    - Random walk (Hurst ~ 0.5): reduce position sizes
(d) Require 2/3 signal agreement before trade: ML prediction + momentum/mean-rev
    confirmation + sentiment gate
(e) Track ensemble agreement rate as a meta-confidence signal

7. NOBEL PRIZE THEORIES FOR ALGORITHMIC TRADING
================================================================================

KEY FINDING: Several Nobel-winning theories have direct practical applications.
The most actionable are: Markowitz (portfolio sizing, already implemented),
Kahneman/Tversky (exploit others' biases), Fama-French (factor exposure),
Thaler (behavioral anomalies as alpha), and Engle (GARCH, already implemented).

SOURCES:

- "When Prospect Theory Meets Mean-Reverting Asset Returns" (ScienceDirect 2024)
  https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0378426624000797
- "Behaviorally Informed DRL for Portfolio Optimization" (Nature Sci Reports 2026)
  https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-026-35902-x
- "From Deep Learning to LLMs: Survey of AI in Quantitative Investment" (arXiv 2025)
  https://arxiv.org/html/2503.21422v1
- "Addressing Estimation Errors through Robust Portfolio Optimization" (Dec 2024)
  https://optimization-online.org/2024/12/addressing-estimation-errors-through-robust-portfolio-optimization/

APPLICABLE NOBEL THEORIES:

A. MARKOWITZ - MODERN PORTFOLIO THEORY (1990 Nobel):
   - Bot already implements correlation-aware sizing (portfolio.py)
   - CRITICAL LIMITATION: Markowitz is extremely sensitive to estimation errors
   - Even with shrinkage estimators, optimization fails with correlated assets
   - "An algorithm that treats point estimates as if known with certainty will
     react to tiny return differences well within measurement error" (error
     maximization)
   - 2024 solution: robust diagonal estimation-error matrices outperform classical
   - Recommended: Ledoit-Wolf shrinkage or resampled efficient frontier

B. FAMA - EFFICIENT MARKET HYPOTHESIS (2013 Nobel):
   - Markets efficient in AGGREGATE but exploitable at EDGES
   - Small-cap, after-hours, crypto markets less efficient
   - Algo trading exploits information processing speed advantage
   - Implication: focus on LESS efficient markets/timeframes for alpha

C. KAHNEMAN & TVERSKY - PROSPECT THEORY (2002 Nobel):
   - Loss aversion: people feel losses 2x more than equivalent gains
   - Disposition effect: hold losers too long, sell winners too early
   - Bot can EXPLOIT: buy when retail panic-sells (loss aversion overshoot)
   - Bot can AVOID: systematic trading removes emotional bias
   - 2025 research: algo trading "minimizes effects of certain emotional biases"
     but does NOT eliminate reference dependence
   - 2026 paper: DRL with loss aversion penalty improves portfolio optimization

D. THALER - BEHAVIORAL ECONOMICS (2017 Nobel):
   - Endowment effect, mental accounting, nudge theory
   - Market anomalies from behavioral biases = trading opportunities
   - Calendar effects persist because of behavioral patterns
   - Bot already captures some via calendar features in indicators.py

E. SHILLER - IRRATIONAL EXUBERANCE (2013 Nobel):
   - CAPE ratio as long-term valuation signal
   - Bot already uses CAPE via macro_indicators.py
   - Excess volatility beyond fundamentals = mean-reversion opportunity

F. ENGLE - ARCH/GARCH (2003 Nobel):
   - Volatility clustering is a proven market phenomenon
   - Bot already uses GARCH(1,1) via volatility.py
   - Consider EGARCH for asymmetric volatility response (bad news = more vol)

ACTION ITEMS FOR THE BOT:
(a) Add robust covariance estimation to portfolio.py
    - Ledoit-Wolf shrinkage: sklearn.covariance.LedoitWolf
    - Or minimum covariance determinant estimator
(b) Add sentiment extremes detector: when sentiment is extremely negative,
    increase buy signal weight (exploit loss aversion overshoot)
(c) Consider EGARCH instead of GARCH for asymmetric volatility
(d) Add Fama-French factor exposure tracking (market, size, value, momentum)

8. RISK OF RUIN AND DRAWDOWN MANAGEMENT
================================================================================

KEY FINDING: For a $100k paper trading account, risk per trade should be 1-2%.
Maximum drawdown target should be 15-20%. Risk of ruin should be below 5%.
Fixed fractional sizing provides mathematical ruin prevention.

SOURCES:
- "Risk of Ruin: Definitions and Why Account Size Matters" (HeyGoTrade)
  https://www.heygotrade.com/en/blog/risk-of-ruin-definitions
- "Risk of Ruin Calculator for Trading" (BacktestBase)
  https://www.backtestbase.com/education/risk-of-ruin-calculator-trading
- "Drawdown Management" (QuantifiedStrategies)
  https://www.quantifiedstrategies.com/drawdown/
- "Strategies to Limit Drawdown Risk in Trading" (NumberAnalytics)
  https://www.numberanalytics.com/blog/strategies-limit-drawdown-risk-trading

KEY INSIGHTS:

A. RISK OF RUIN MATHEMATICS:
   - Risk of Ruin = ((1 - Edge) / (1 + Edge)) ^ (Capital / Risk_Per_Trade)
   - For 55% win rate, 1:1.5 risk-reward, 2% risk per trade:
     Risk of Ruin ~ 1.3% (acceptable)
   - For 55% win rate, 1:1.5 risk-reward, 5% risk per trade:
     Risk of Ruin ~ 12.6% (too high)
   - Professional target: RoR < 5%, institutional: RoR < 1%

B. DRAWDOWN RECOVERY MATHEMATICS:
   -  10% drawdown: need  11.1% gain to recover
   -  20% drawdown: need  25.0% gain to recover
   -  30% drawdown: need  42.9% gain to recover
   -  50% drawdown: need 100.0% gain to recover
   - Relationship is exponential -- drawdowns beyond 25% are devastating

C. RECOMMENDED RISK PARAMETERS ($100k account):
   - Max risk per trade: 1-2% ($1,000-$2,000)
   - Max correlated risk: 6% (e.g., 3 tech stocks at 2% each)
   - Max total portfolio risk: 20% of capital
   - Max drawdown target: 15-20%
   - Daily loss limit: 3-5% ($3,000-$5,000)
   - If drawdown hits 15%, reduce position sizes by 50%
   - If drawdown hits 25%, halt trading, reassess

D. FIXED FRACTIONAL AS SAFETY NET:
   - Risk fixed % of CURRENT balance (not initial capital)
   - As account shrinks, dollar risk shrinks (mathematical brake)
   - Impossible to reach $0 (asymptotic approach)
   - Small accounts: 0.5% risk per trade maximum for survival

ACTION ITEMS FOR THE BOT:
(a) Add risk-of-ruin calculation to monitoring dashboard
(b) Implement drawdown-based position scaling:
    - Drawdown 0-10%: normal sizing
    - Drawdown 10-15%: reduce by 25%
    - Drawdown 15-20%: reduce by 50%
    - Drawdown >20%: reduce by 75%, alert operator
(c) Add daily loss limit: if daily P&L < -3%, stop trading for the day
(d) Track risk per trade and ensure Kelly sizing never exceeds 2% of equity
(e) Add max correlated risk check in portfolio.py

9. MARKET MICROSTRUCTURE FOR RETAIL TRADERS
================================================================================

KEY FINDING: Spread costs and slippage are the biggest hidden costs for retail
algo traders. Limit orders reduce costs but risk non-fill. The bot's existing
spread-aware ordering is a competitive advantage.

SOURCES:
- "Six Market Microstructure Research Papers You Must Read" (Global Trading 2024)
  https://www.globaltrading.net/research-on-the-web-in-2024/
- "TCA: Benchmarks and Slippage" (Talos 2024)
  https://www.talos.com/insights/execution-insights-through-transaction-cost-analysis-tca-benchmarks-and-slippage
- "Market Microstructure Trading" (QuantVPS 2025)
  https://www.quantvps.com/blog/market-micro-structure-trading
- "Stop Trading Like It's 2010" (Bookmap 2025)
  https://bookmap.com/blog/stop-trading-like-its-2010-whats-changed-in-the-market-microstructure
- "Market Microstructure Guide" (AboveTheGreenLine)
  https://abovethegreenline.com/market-microstructure/

KEY INSIGHTS:

A. SPREAD COSTS FOR RETAIL:
   - Average equity spread: 1-3 cents for liquid stocks, 5-20 cents for illiquid
   - Crypto spread: 5-50 bps on major exchanges, 50-200 bps on illiquid pairs
   - Round-trip cost: spread is paid TWICE (buy and sell)
   - For a $1,000 trade on a stock with 5 cent spread ($50 stock):
     Cost = 2 * (0.05/50) = 0.2% round trip
   - This alone can destroy a strategy with <1% expected return per trade

B. SLIPPAGE SOURCES:
   - Price movement between signal and execution
   - Partial fills at worse prices (order book depth)
   - Queue position for limit orders
   - Latency (less relevant for daily/hourly strategies)

C. ORDER TYPE OPTIMIZATION:
   - Market orders: guaranteed fill, uncertain price
   - Limit orders at mid: save half spread, risk non-fill
   - TWAP/VWAP: split large orders over time, reduce market impact
   - For daily strategy: limit at slightly better than current price, with
     market order fallback after timeout

D. 2025 MARKET EVOLUTION:
   - Dark pools hide significant institutional liquidity
   - Payment for order flow: retail orders routed to market makers
   - Retail limit orders may get adversely selected
   - Automation and speed define advantage even for retail

ACTION ITEMS FOR THE BOT:
(a) The bot already has spread-aware limit pricing (order_utils.py) -- keep it
(b) Add spread tracking: log actual spread at trade time, compute total spread
    cost over time. Use this to refine backtest cost assumptions
(c) Implement adaptive order timeout: wider spread = longer limit order patience
(d) Add order book depth check before large orders (if API supports)
(e) For crypto: add exchange spread comparison before routing
(f) Track fill rate and slippage metrics to monitor execution quality

KEY DIFFERENCES:

A. MICROSTRUCTURE METRICS:
   - Roll Measure: "surprisingly high" in crypto vs equities
   - VPIN: higher in crypto = greater toxicity / information asymmetry
   - Greater serial correlation = more momentum-based trading works
   - Implication: momentum strategies work BETTER in crypto than equities

B. LIQUIDITY STRUCTURE:
   - Crypto: fragmented across 100+ exchanges and DEXs
   - No best-execution regulation (unlike equity Reg NMS)
   - BTC depth: $2.71M at 21:00 UTC vs $3.86M at 11:00 UTC (42% drop overnight)
   - Weekday peak: Tuesday-Thursday (institutional/ETF activity)
   - Saturday: lowest spot volume
   - Implication: trade during high-liquidity hours, reduce weekend sizing

C. BITCOIN ETF IMPACT (2024-2025):
   - Reduced BTC volatility by 55%
   - Cumulative net inflows >$52.9 billion through Sep 2025
   - Liquidity increasingly concentrated during US market hours
   - Crypto becoming MORE like equity market structure
   - Weekend/holiday liquidity gaps amplified (ETF bridge pauses)

D. MARKET EFFICIENCY:
   - Rising liquidity + declining volatility = increasing efficiency
   - During uncertainty: inefficiencies EXACERBATED among illiquid crypto
   - Less liquid alts: "often untradeable outside of majors"
   - Stick to high-liquidity pairs (BTC, ETH) for algo trading

E. STRUCTURAL RISKS UNIQUE TO CRYPTO:
   - No circuit breakers
   - Exchange counterparty risk (not centrally cleared)
   - Smart contract risk for DEX trades
   - Stablecoin peg risk (bot already monitors this)

(b) Add time-of-day liquidity filter for crypto:
    - Prefer trading during US market hours (highest liquidity post-ETF)
    - Reduce position sizes during off-peak hours (especially weekends)
(c) Add VPIN calculation as a feature/risk signal for crypto
(d) Monitor BTC ETF flows as a sentiment/flow signal
(e) Consider separate Optuna hypersearches for crypto vs stock parameters
