1. MODEL ARCHITECTURES BEYOND LSTM
================================================================================

Our current model (model_v2.py) is a 2-layer LSTM with MultiheadAttention,
residual connection, LayerNorm, and a 2-layer FC head predicting continuous
returns. Input: (batch, seq_len, input_dim) where seq_len is typically 12-32
hourly bars and input_dim is ~25-35 features. The model is JIT-traced for
~30% faster inference on Jetson. Training uses Huber loss with return-weighted
emphasis and is optimized via Sharpe ratio across walk-forward folds.

Below we evaluate seven alternative architectures.

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1.1  TEMPORAL FUSION TRANSFORMER (TFT)
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
What it is:
Google's TFT (Lim et al., 2019) combines LSTM for local temporal processing
with interpretable multi-head self-attention for long-range dependencies. Key
innovations include: Variable Selection Networks (VSN) that learn which inputs
matter at each timestep, Gated Residual Networks (GRN) for nonlinear processing,
and interpretable multi-horizon forecasting.

Why it works:
TFT excels because it explicitly handles three types of inputs: (1) static
metadata (e.g., asset type), (2) known future inputs (e.g., day-of-week), and
(3) observed past inputs (e.g., prices, indicators). The Variable Selection
Network automatically learns feature importance, providing interpretability that
pure LSTMs lack. Recent crypto studies (2024-2025) show TFT outperforming LSTM
baselines on multi-asset forecasting when combined with on-chain indicators.

How to integrate into our system:
- Replace RegressionLSTM in model_v2.py with a TFT implementation
- Categorize our features into static covariates (asset type), known future
  (Hour_sin/cos, Day_sin/cos), and observed (all indicators)
- Use pytorch-forecasting library or implement a custom lightweight version
- Keep the same training pipeline (hypersearch_v2.py) with minimal changes

Pros vs our LSTM:
+ Automatic feature selection (interpretable, which features drive predictions)
+ Multi-horizon forecasting natively (our multi-horizon targets fit perfectly)
+ Handles static metadata (could encode asset-specific information)
+ Better at capturing long-range dependencies than LSTM alone
+ Well-studied for financial applications

Cons vs our LSTM:
- Significantly more parameters (2-5x our current model size)
- Longer training time (2-4x)
- More complex to implement and debug
- Overfitting risk with small datasets is higher
- JIT tracing may be more fragile due to complex control flow

Feasibility on Jetson Orin Nano:
MODERATE. A lightweight TFT with hidden_dim=64 and 2 attention heads would fit
in 8GB unified memory. Full TFT (hidden_dim=256, 8 heads) may OOM during
training. Inference is feasible with FP16. Estimated inference time: 3-5ms per
prediction (vs ~1-2ms for current LSTM).

Implementation complexity: HARD
Expected impact: MODERATE-HIGH (10-25% Sharpe improvement based on literature)

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1.2  N-BEATS / N-HiTS
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
What it is:
N-BEATS (Oreshkin et al., 2019) is a purely feed-forward architecture based on
backward and forward residual links (doubly residual stacking). It uses FC
blocks organized into stacks, where each block predicts basis expansion
coefficients both forward (forecast) and backward (backcast). N-HiTS extends
this with multi-rate input sampling for hierarchical temporal decomposition.

Why it works:
N-BEATS achieved 11% improvement over statistical baselines and 3% over the
M4 competition winner. Its strength is decomposing time series into trend and
seasonality components through interpretable basis functions. N-HiTS adds
hierarchical pooling to capture patterns at multiple time scales.

How to integrate:
- These are univariate models by design. We would need to either:
  (a) Use N-BEATS for each feature independently, then combine predictions
  (b) Adapt the architecture for multivariate input (non-trivial)
- Better suited as an ensemble member alongside our LSTM rather than a
  replacement
- Could use the nixtla/neuralforecast library implementation

Pros vs our LSTM:
+ Interpretable decomposition (trend vs seasonality)
+ No recurrence = parallelizable = faster training
+ Strong benchmark results on standard time series tasks
+ Simpler architecture (just FC layers)

Cons vs our LSTM:
- Designed for univariate forecasting (not multivariate features)
- No attention mechanism for cross-feature interaction
- Less suitable for our multi-feature, multi-asset setup
- Financial time series results are mixed compared to benchmarks

Feasibility on Jetson:
HIGH. Pure FC architecture is very lightweight. N-BEATS with 30 blocks would
use <100MB memory. Training and inference both fast.

Implementation complexity: MEDIUM (as ensemble member), HARD (as multivariate)
Expected impact: LOW-MODERATE (5-15% if used in ensemble)

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1.3  PatchTST
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
What it is:
PatchTST (Nie et al., 2023, ICLR) segments time series into subseries-level
patches served as input tokens to a Transformer. Two key design choices:
(1) patching retains local semantic information while quadratically reducing
attention computation, and (2) channel-independence where each univariate
channel shares the same Transformer weights.

Why it works:
Patching creates meaningful tokens (like words in NLP) from contiguous time
segments rather than individual timesteps. This allows the model to attend to
longer history with less compute. Results: 21% MSE reduction and 16.7% MAE
reduction over previous Transformer-based models on standard benchmarks.

How to integrate:
- Replace our LSTM encoder with a PatchTST encoder
- Our seq_len=24 could be patched into 4 patches of 6 or 6 patches of 4
- Keep our FC head for regression output
- Channel-independence means each feature is processed separately (efficient
  but loses cross-feature interaction)

Pros vs our LSTM:
+ Much more efficient attention (quadratic reduction via patching)
+ Better long-range dependency modeling
+ Proven strong results on time series benchmarks
+ Parallelizable (no sequential processing like LSTM)
+ Recent FP32 quantization work (KDD 2025) makes it edge-friendly

Cons vs our LSTM:
- Channel-independence loses cross-feature correlations
- Relatively new for financial applications
- Patch size is another hyperparameter to tune
- May not benefit much with our short sequences (12-32 bars)

Feasibility on Jetson:
HIGH. PatchTST is more efficient than standard Transformers. With patches,
our seq_len=24 becomes only 4-6 tokens, making attention trivial. FP16
inference would be very fast.

Implementation complexity: MEDIUM
Expected impact: MODERATE (10-20% on time series benchmarks, unclear for finance)

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1.4  TimesNet
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
What it is:
TimesNet (Wu et al., 2023, ICLR) extends temporal analysis to 2D by
transforming 1D time series into 2D tensors based on detected periodicities.
Intraperiod variations map to columns, interperiod variations to rows, then
standard 2D convolutions (like image CNNs) capture both types of patterns.

Why it works:
Financial time series have multiple periodicities (hourly, daily, weekly) that
interact. TimesNet captures these through 2D convolution, achieving SOTA on
five mainstream tasks. For financial volatility forecasting, it showed
competitive RMSE and QLIKE performance.

How to integrate:
- Replace our LSTM with a TimesNet encoder
- Requires FFT-based period detection as a preprocessing step
- Keep our FC regression head

Pros vs our LSTM:
+ Captures multi-periodic patterns naturally
+ General-purpose (also handles anomaly detection, classification)
+ 2D CNN backbone is well-optimized on GPU

Cons vs our LSTM:
- Mixed results in financial MAE/MAPE metrics
- FFT-based periodicity detection adds complexity
- 2D reshaping of 1D data is somewhat artificial
- May not improve on our attention-augmented LSTM

Feasibility on Jetson:
MODERATE. 2D convolutions are well-supported on Jetson GPUs. However, the
Inception-style backbone is moderately parameter-heavy. Should fit in 8GB
with careful sizing.

Implementation complexity: HARD
Expected impact: LOW-MODERATE (competitive but not clearly superior for finance)

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1.5  MAMBA / STATE SPACE MODELS (SSM)
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
What it is:
Mamba (Gu & Dao, 2023) is a selective state space model that achieves
linear-time sequence processing (O(n) vs O(n^2) for attention). Key innovation:
input-dependent selection mechanism that dynamically focuses on relevant parts
of the input, similar to attention but without quadratic cost.

Why it works:
For financial time series, Mamba models (MambaStock, CryptoMamba, CMDMamba)
have shown strong results in 2024-2025. MambaLLM accurately forecasts through
the volatile late 2024 to early 2025 period where LSTM baselines lagged.
CMDMamba achieves near-linear time complexity for efficient forecasting.

How to integrate:
- Replace LSTM with Mamba backbone: input -> Mamba layers -> pooling -> FC head
- Use the mamba-ssm library or causal-conv1d implementation
- Keep the same training pipeline and feature set

Pros vs our LSTM:
+ Linear time complexity (much faster for long sequences)
+ Strong empirical results on financial time series
+ Better at capturing long-range dependencies
+ Lower memory usage than attention-based models
+ Active research community, rapidly improving

Cons vs our LSTM:
- Relatively new architecture (less battle-tested)
- Custom CUDA kernels may need adaptation for sm_87
- JIT tracing compatibility unknown
- Fewer pretrained models available
- Our sequences are short (12-32 bars), so linear vs quadratic doesn't matter

Feasibility on Jetson:
UNCERTAIN. Mamba's custom CUDA kernels (selective scan) may need recompilation
for sm_87. The causal-conv1d library might not have ARM64 wheels. If the CUDA
kernels compile successfully, it would be very efficient. If not, a pure PyTorch
fallback implementation exists but is slower.

Implementation complexity: MEDIUM-HARD (depends on CUDA kernel compatibility)
Expected impact: MODERATE-HIGH (strong recent results in crypto/stock prediction)

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1.6  TEMPORAL CONVOLUTIONAL NETWORK (TCN)
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
What it is:
TCN uses dilated causal convolutions to capture temporal patterns. Each layer
uses increasing dilation rates (1, 2, 4, 8...) to expand the receptive field
exponentially. Causal convolutions ensure no future information leaks. Combined
with residual connections, dropout, and weight normalization.

Why it works:
TCN outperforms LSTM in many financial benchmarks (stock prediction, volatility
estimation, value-at-risk). The dilated convolutions efficiently capture both
short-term and long-term dependencies. Parallelizable during training (no
sequential bottleneck). Recent 2024 studies show TCN with attention achieving
MAE of 1.23-1.45 on stock data, outperforming LSTM and ARIMA.

How to integrate:
- Replace LSTM with TCN backbone: input -> TCN blocks -> FC head
- TCN blocks: dilated causal conv1d -> WeightNorm -> ReLU -> Dropout -> residual
- Use pytorch-tcn library or implement from scratch (relatively simple)
- Keep our attention mechanism on top of TCN output

Pros vs our LSTM:
+ Fully parallelizable (faster training)
+ Better gradient flow (no vanishing gradients)
+ Explicit control over receptive field via dilation
+ Lower memory usage during training
+ Simple to implement and debug
+ Well-suited for our fixed-length sequences

Cons vs our LSTM:
- Fixed receptive field (not adaptive like attention)
- Requires careful dilation schedule tuning
- May need deeper networks for very long-range patterns
- Less interpretable than attention weights

Feasibility on Jetson:
HIGH. Conv1d operations are heavily optimized on CUDA. TCN is lighter than
LSTM for both training and inference. JIT tracing works perfectly for pure
convolution architectures. FP16 inference is straightforward.

Implementation complexity: EASY
Expected impact: MODERATE (likely similar or slightly better than LSTM)
RECOMMENDED as a quick experiment due to easy implementation.

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1.7  ENSEMBLE: XGBoost/LightGBM + NEURAL NETWORK STACKING
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
What it is:
Gradient boosted trees (XGBoost, LightGBM) with time series features as a
first-stage predictor, stacked with neural network predictions in a
meta-learner. The tree models capture non-linear feature interactions that
the neural network may miss, while the neural network captures sequential
patterns.

Why it works:
Research consistently shows 10-15% improvement from stacking tree-based and
deep learning models vs either alone. Tree models excel at tabular data with
diverse feature types. They naturally handle missing values, require minimal
preprocessing, and are fast to train. The stacking approach combines
complementary strengths.

How to integrate:
Step 1: Train LightGBM on our existing features (flatten sequence into features
        like RSI_t, RSI_t-1, RSI_t-2, etc.)
Step 2: Use LightGBM prediction as an additional feature for our LSTM
Step 3: Meta-learner (linear or small neural net) combines both predictions
- Can also use LightGBM for feature importance analysis to prune our feature set

Pros vs our LSTM alone:
+ Captures non-linear feature interactions trees excel at
+ Fast training (minutes vs hours for neural nets)
+ No sequence length constraint (can use arbitrary lookback features)
+ Excellent interpretability (SHAP values, feature importance)
+ Robust to noisy features (built-in regularization)
+ Proven 10-15% accuracy improvement in stacking

Cons:
- More complex pipeline (two models to maintain)
- Need to flatten sequential data for tree input
- Risk of overfitting the meta-learner
- Feature engineering for tree model is different than for LSTM

Feasibility on Jetson:
VERY HIGH. LightGBM is CPU-only and extremely fast on ARM64. Training on our
dataset would take seconds to minutes. Inference is sub-millisecond. Memory
usage is minimal.

Implementation complexity: EASY-MEDIUM
Expected impact: MODERATE-HIGH (10-15% improvement based on consistent literature)
HIGHLY RECOMMENDED. Easy win with well-proven results.

2.4  STATISTICAL / REGIME FEATURES
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Hurst Exponent:
- Measures tendency to trend (H > 0.5) vs mean-revert (H < 0.5)
- Rolling Hurst over 100-bar window tells you WHICH strategy to use
- H > 0.5: trend-following signals are reliable
- H < 0.5: mean-reversion signals are reliable
- H = 0.5: random walk, no edge
- Integration: Use nolds library or implement rescaled range analysis
- Add as rolling feature: rolling_hurst_100

Fractal Dimension:
- D = 2 - H (directly from Hurst exponent)
- D close to 1: smooth trend, D close to 2: rough/noisy
- Integration: Derived from Hurst, no additional computation

Entropy Measures:
- Approximate Entropy (ApEn): measures predictability of time series
- Sample Entropy (SampEn): improved version of ApEn
- Low entropy = more predictable = stronger signals
- Integration: Rolling 50-bar entropy as a feature

Regime Detection via Hidden Markov Models:
- HMM with 2-3 states: bull/bear/neutral or high-vol/low-vol
- Train HMM on returns and volatility
- Use current regime state as a feature for the LSTM
- Integration: Fit HMM offline, use regime probability as feature
- Library: hmmlearn

Markov Switching Model:
- Similar to HMM but parameters switch between regimes
- Captures different return/volatility dynamics in each regime
- Integration: Use statsmodels MarkovSwitching or regime-switching GARCH

Implementation complexity: EASY (Hurst/entropy), MEDIUM (HMM/Markov switching)
Expected impact: HIGH. Regime detection is consistently cited as one of the
most impactful improvements for trading systems. Knowing whether the market is
trending or mean-reverting changes which signals to trust.
HIGHLY RECOMMENDED. Hurst exponent is easy to add and highly informative.

3.1  KELLY CRITERION — Optimal Position Sizing
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

What it is:
The Kelly Criterion calculates the optimal fraction of capital to risk on each
trade to maximize long-term growth rate. For a trade with probability p of
winning and odds b (reward/risk ratio):
    f* = p - (1-p)/b = (p*b - (1-p)) / b

Practical application:
- Use our model's historical accuracy to estimate p (win rate)
- Use our mean win / mean loss to estimate b
- Apply fractional Kelly (1/2 or 1/3 Kelly) to reduce volatility

How to integrate:
1. Track rolling win rate and win/loss ratio from trade_memory
2. Compute Kelly fraction: kelly_f = (win_rate * avg_win/avg_loss - (1-win_rate)) / (avg_win/avg_loss)
3. Use half-Kelly: position_size = 0.5 * kelly_f * equity
4. Replace fixed NOTIONAL_PER_SYMBOL with Kelly-derived sizing
5. Maintain minimum/maximum position bounds for safety

Example: If win_rate=0.55, avg_win=2%, avg_loss=1.5% (b=1.33):
  kelly_f = (0.55*1.33 - 0.45)/1.33 = 0.21 (21% of equity per trade)
  half_kelly = 10.5% of equity per trade
  On $100k account: ~$10,500 per trade (vs current $1000-$5000)

Implementation complexity: EASY
Expected impact: MODERATE-HIGH (optimal growth rate, reduced max drawdown)
HIGHLY RECOMMENDED. Can be implemented in a few hours.

NOTE: Kelly requires accurate estimates. With inaccurate win rate estimates,
full Kelly can be disastrous. Always use fractional Kelly (1/3 to 1/2) and
implement a minimum sample size (e.g., 50 trades) before switching from fixed
sizing to Kelly.

3.2  RISK PARITY
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

What it is:
Allocate by risk contribution rather than capital. Each asset contributes equal
risk to the portfolio. An asset with half the volatility gets double the
allocation.

How to integrate:
- Compute rolling volatility for each symbol (we already have Volatility_12h)
- Weight inversely by volatility: w_i = (1/vol_i) / sum(1/vol_j)
- Multiply by total target exposure to get per-symbol allocation

Integration path:
In crypto_loop.py and stock_loop.py, replace fixed NOTIONAL_PER_SYMBOL with
volatility-weighted allocation:
  vol_i = rolling_std(returns, 24)  # 24-bar rolling volatility
  inv_vol = 1.0 / vol_i
  weights = inv_vol / inv_vol.sum()
  notional_i = TOTAL_NOTIONAL * weights[i]

Implementation complexity: EASY
Expected impact: MODERATE (better risk-adjusted returns, smoother equity curve)

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3.3  BLACK-LITTERMAN MODEL
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

What it is:
Combines market equilibrium returns (from CAPM or market cap weights) with
subjective views (our ML predictions) to produce posterior expected returns
for portfolio optimization. Solves the problem of overly concentrated
Markowitz portfolios by anchoring to market equilibrium.

How to integrate:
Our ML predictions are perfect "views" for Black-Litterman:
- Market-implied returns = market cap weighted equilibrium
- Views = our predicted returns for each symbol
- Confidence = based on model prediction strength
- Use PyPortfolioOpt library (BlackLittermanModel class)
- Output: optimal portfolio weights incorporating both market consensus and
  our model's views

Integration path:
1. Install pypfopt
2. Before the buy/sell loop, compute BL-optimal weights using predictions
3. Use BL weights instead of equal-weight allocation
4. This naturally handles the "top N selection" problem more elegantly

Implementation complexity: MEDIUM
Expected impact: HIGH (Black-Litterman is the industry standard for
  incorporating views into portfolio construction)
RECOMMENDED, especially for stock portfolio.

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3.4  CVaR / EXPECTED SHORTFALL
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

What it is:
Conditional Value at Risk (CVaR) measures the expected loss in the worst X%
of scenarios. Unlike VaR (which just gives a threshold), CVaR tells you the
average loss when things go bad. It is a coherent risk measure (sub-additive)
unlike VaR.

How to integrate:
- Compute rolling CVaR for each position using historical returns
- Use as a risk constraint: reduce position if CVaR exceeds threshold
- Replace our fixed stop-loss percentages with CVaR-based limits

Computing CVaR:
  returns = sorted(historical_returns)
  var_5pct = np.percentile(returns, 5)
  cvar_5pct = returns[returns <= var_5pct].mean()
  If abs(cvar_5pct) > MAX_ACCEPTABLE_LOSS: reduce_position()

Implementation complexity: EASY
Expected impact: MODERATE (better tail risk management than fixed stops)

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3.5  REGIME-BASED ALLOCATION
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

What it is:
Adjust strategy parameters based on detected market regime:
- Bull/trending regime: larger positions, wider stops, trend-following signals
- Bear/crisis regime: smaller positions, tighter stops, defensive
- High-volatility regime: reduce all positions, widen stops proportionally
- Low-volatility regime: normal positions, tighter stops

How to integrate:
1. Detect regime using HMM (section 2.4) or simple rules:
   - Bull: SPY > SMA200 and VIX < 20
   - Bear: SPY < SMA200 or VIX > 30
   - High-vol: ATR percentile > 80th
2. Adjust NOTIONAL_PER_SYMBOL, stop distances, and trade_threshold based on
   current regime
3. In crisis regime, increase CIRCUIT_BREAKER_PCT sensitivity

Example regime adjustments:
  if regime == 'bull':
      notional_multiplier = 1.2
      trade_threshold_adj = 0.9   # lower threshold = more trades
  elif regime == 'high_vol':
      notional_multiplier = 0.5
      trade_threshold_adj = 1.5   # higher threshold = fewer trades
  elif regime == 'bear':
      notional_multiplier = 0.3
      trade_threshold_adj = 2.0   # much higher threshold

Implementation complexity: MEDIUM
Expected impact: HIGH (regime-awareness is one of the highest-impact improvements)
HIGHLY RECOMMENDED in combination with Hurst exponent from section 2.4.

4. PROVEN QUANTITATIVE STRATEGIES
================================================================================

Our current strategy is fundamentally a directional prediction strategy:
predict whether an asset will go up or down, then buy or sell. Below are
additional quantitative strategies that could complement or replace this.

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4.1  STATISTICAL ARBITRAGE / PAIRS TRADING
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

What it is:
Identify pairs of assets that are cointegrated (long-term equilibrium
relationship). When the spread between them deviates from the mean, bet on
convergence: go long the underperformer and short the outperformer.

Core methodology:
1. Test all pairs for cointegration (Engle-Granger or Johansen test)
2. For cointegrated pairs, compute the spread and its z-score
3. Enter trade when z-score > 2 (or < -2): long the cheap one, short the expensive
4. Exit when z-score reverts to 0

Crypto application:
- BTC/ETH is a natural pair with strong cointegration historically
- Altcoin pairs within similar categories (e.g., SOL/AVAX, LINK/UNI)
- Recent 2024-2025 research shows deep learning for co-integrated crypto pair
  prediction outperforms traditional methods

How to integrate:
- Use statsmodels coint() to test pairs from our symbol universe
- Add a pairs trading mode alongside our directional strategy
- Compute z-scores and trade the spread
- Requires ability to short (check Alpaca crypto shorting support)

4.3  MEAN REVERSION — Ornstein-Uhlenbeck Process
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

What it is:
Model asset prices (or spreads) as an Ornstein-Uhlenbeck (OU) process:
  dX = theta * (mu - X) * dt + sigma * dW
where theta is speed of reversion, mu is long-term mean, sigma is volatility.

Key metric: Half-life of mean reversion
  half_life = -ln(2) / ln(1 + theta)
  or estimated via regression: dX_t = a + b * X_{t-1} + epsilon_t
  half_life = -ln(2) / b

How to integrate:
1. Test if each symbol's price-SMA spread is mean-reverting (ADF test)
2. Estimate half-life from regression
3. If half-life is reasonable (5-50 bars), trade mean reversion
4. Entry: spread deviates > 2 std from mean; exit: spread reverts to mean

Practical application:
- Use Bollinger Band %B (already in our features) as mean reversion signal
- When %B < 0 (below lower band) and Hurst < 0.5: mean reversion buy signal
- When %B > 1 (above upper band) and Hurst < 0.5: mean reversion sell signal

Implementation complexity: MEDIUM
Expected impact: MODERATE (works well in range-bound markets, complements
trend-following in trending markets)

6. BACKTESTING & VALIDATION
================================================================================

Our current approach (hypersearch_v2.py):
- Walk-forward cross-validation with expanding windows (3 folds)
- Embargo gap (seq_len * 1) between train and validation
- Sharpe ratio as optimization objective
- Risk-adjusted score: mean_sharpe - 0.5 * std_sharpe
- Optuna TPE sampler with median pruning
- Per-fold scaler fitting (no data leakage)

This is already quite good. Below are improvements.

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6.1  COMBINATORIAL PURGED CROSS-VALIDATION (CPCV)
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

What it is:
De Prado's CPCV (from "Advances in Financial Machine Learning") generates ALL
possible combinations of train/test splits from N groups, producing multiple
backtest paths. Combined with purging (removing overlapping labels) and
embargoing (removing nearby observations), it provides the most robust
estimate of out-of-sample performance.

Why it matters:
Our current 3-fold walk-forward gives only 3 test paths. CPCV with N=5, k=2
produces C(5,2)=10 paths, giving a distribution of performance estimates. This
lets us compute the Probability of Backtest Overfitting (PBO) -- the probability
that the best in-sample model is mediocre out-of-sample.

How to integrate:
- Use skfolio library: CombinatorialPurgedCV class
- Replace get_walk_forward_folds() with CPCV fold generator
- Compute PBO and Deflated Sharpe Ratio (DSR) as additional metrics
- Flag models with PBO > 0.5 as likely overfit

Implementation details:
  from skfolio.model_selection import CombinatorialPurgedCV
  cv = CombinatorialPurgedCV(
      n_folds=6, n_test_folds=2,
      purge_timedelta=pd.Timedelta(hours=seq_len),
      embargo_timedelta=pd.Timedelta(hours=seq_len),
  )
  for train_idx, test_idx in cv.split(X):
      # train and evaluate

Implementation complexity: MEDIUM
Expected impact: HIGH (dramatically reduces probability of deploying overfit models)
HIGHLY RECOMMENDED. This is the industry standard for quant backtesting validation.

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6.2  MONTE CARLO SIMULATION
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

What it is:
Test strategy robustness by reshuffling trade returns and examining the
distribution of outcomes. If the strategy is robust, most reshuffled paths
should still be profitable.

How to integrate:
After walk-forward evaluation:
1. Collect all trade returns from validation folds
2. Resample (with replacement) 10,000 times to create randomized paths
3. Compute Sharpe ratio for each path
4. If 95th percentile Sharpe > 0: strategy is robust
5. Report: median Sharpe, 5th percentile Sharpe, probability of positive Sharpe

Code sketch:
  trade_returns = [...]  # from validation
  n_sims = 10000
  sharpes = []
  for _ in range(n_sims):
      shuffled = np.random.choice(trade_returns, size=len(trade_returns), replace=True)
      sharpes.append(shuffled.mean() / shuffled.std() * np.sqrt(252))
  pct_5 = np.percentile(sharpes, 5)
  prob_positive = np.mean(np.array(sharpes) > 0)

Implementation complexity: EASY
Expected impact: MODERATE (confidence measure for strategy robustness)
RECOMMENDED. Easy to add to the training pipeline.

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6.3  REGIME-AWARE BACKTESTING
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

What it is:
Split the test set into regime-specific segments and evaluate performance in
each regime separately. A strategy that works in bull markets but fails in
bear markets is fragile.

How to integrate:
After validation:
1. Label each test bar as bull/bear/sideways using SMA200 or returns
2. Compute Sharpe ratio in each regime
3. Reject models that have negative Sharpe in any regime
4. Weight the score: score = min(bull_sharpe, bear_sharpe, sideways_sharpe)
   (optimistic version: weighted average with minimum constraint)

Enhancement to hypersearch_v2.py:
  # In compute_sharpe or after final evaluation:
  bull_mask = actual_returns > 0.1  # etc.
  bear_mask = actual_returns < -0.1
  bull_sharpe = compute_sharpe(predictions[bull_mask], actual_returns[bull_mask], threshold)
  bear_sharpe = compute_sharpe(predictions[bear_mask], actual_returns[bear_mask], threshold)
  min_regime_sharpe = min(bull_sharpe, bear_sharpe)
  # Penalize models that only work in one regime

Implementation complexity: EASY
Expected impact: MODERATE-HIGH (prevents deploying regime-fragile models)
RECOMMENDED. Trivial to add as a post-evaluation metric.

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6.4  TRANSACTION COST MODELING
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Our current approach only checks if predicted return > 2x spread. More
realistic cost modeling should include:

- Round-trip spread cost: already modeled
- Slippage: the difference between expected and actual fill price
  Estimate: 1-2 bps for stocks, 2-5 bps for crypto
- Market impact: for our order sizes, negligible
  But include: 0-1 bps for stocks, 0-2 bps for crypto
- Opportunity cost: trades we miss due to latency or liquidity

Integration into training:
In compute_sharpe(), subtract estimated transaction costs:
  trade_returns = signals[traded] * actual_returns[traded]
  # Subtract round-trip cost for each trade
  cost_per_trade = 0.05  # 5 bps = 0.05%
  trade_returns -= cost_per_trade
  # This makes the Sharpe more realistic

Implementation complexity: EASY
Expected impact: MODERATE (prevents over-trading on marginal signals)
RECOMMENDED. Should be standard practice.

SUMMARY
================================================================================

Our current system is well-architected with good fundamentals:
- LSTM + MultiheadAttention is a competitive architecture
- Walk-forward CV with Sharpe optimization is solid methodology
- ATR-based stops, sentiment gating, and LLM oversight are strong risk controls
- The feature set covers major technical indicators and cross-asset signals

The highest-impact improvements, in order of expected ROI:

1. ADD REGIME DETECTION (Hurst exponent + HMM) — transforms the system from
   always-on to context-aware. Easy to implement, proven high impact.

2. LIGHTGBM ENSEMBLE — stacking tree models with neural net predictions
   consistently adds 10-15%. Well-proven, moderate effort.

3. KELLY CRITERION SIZING — optimal position sizing based on actual edge.
   Easy to implement, mathematically optimal.

4. CPCV VALIDATION — prevents deploying overfit models. Industry standard.

5. BLACK-LITTERMAN PORTFOLIO — institutional-grade weight optimization using
   our ML predictions as views. Moderate effort, high impact for stock portfolio.

6. TCN EXPERIMENT — quick architectural experiment. If TCN matches or beats
   LSTM, we gain faster training. If not, minimal wasted effort.

The LEAST promising paths for our system:
- Market making (requires sub-second latency we don't have)
- Full GNN (our asset graph is too small)
- TimesNet (mixed financial results)
- TWAP/VWAP (our order sizes are too small to matter)
